Agents that act for different people have to coordinate. We measure whether they can.

MAC-Bench places language-model agents in three everyday service settings. Each agent holds private information and its own permissions. Every booking, payment and settlement goes through a simulated service, and a program scores the final state.

Design

Four rules the benchmark is built on

The world keeps the truth

Proposals, orders, authorizations and settlements are objects in a simulated service. A price written in a chat message commits nobody. The verifier reads the final state and the event log, and scores with code.

Roles are seats

A seat can be driven by the agent under test, a script, a frozen model, the reference policy or a person. The same task serves as a training environment and as a team evaluation.

Consent is an object

An agent cannot charge or cancel on its own. A traveller grants a single-use authorization for one specific proposal or order, with a charge ceiling. If the plan changes afterwards, the authorization no longer matches.

Tasks are Harbor folders

Tasks follow the Harbor task format used by Terminal-Bench. General tools come from the agent's own harness. The environment adds a domain service over MCP and REST, and a message board. Every ticketing task now exports to such a folder; none has been run under Harbor yet.

      Tasks

      Forty-six development tasks and a held-out set

      A task fixes a world, two scripted travellers with private facts and plans, and what the airline will do. The booking agent is the seat under test. An episode is judged on the world as it stands at the end: every traveller must have what they wanted within budget, and among such outcomes a lower loss for the travellers is better. One reference policy solves every task. An agent that leaves at once must fail, unless nothing needed doing. Models have played tasks only in first looks outside containers, one or two trials a task; there are no results yet.

      Careless agents

      The reference policy has eight habits, each a switch. Switching one off gives an agent that should fail the tasks that need that habit, and only those. A task is admitted to a set only if the careless agent of its family fails it; a task with two traps, only if every careless agent named for it does. These agents are a check on the tasks. They are not baselines.

      Tasks from a generator

      A recipe per family draws a world and looks in it for a place where the difficulty really occurs: a flight with one seat left at its fare, two cities whose cheapest flights land hours apart, a flight with an earlier one by the same airline. It builds two travellers around that place. Budgets are what a reference run paid, plus a margin of 8 to 25 percent. A candidate becomes a task only if the reference policy solves it, leaving at once fails it, the careless agent of its family fails it, and the pressure it is labelled with is really in it. Every traveller pays for their own ticket and nothing else, so two budgets cannot be pooled. A task in which a traveller adds words is the twin of the same task without them: what the words do to a model is the difference between its results on the two.

      Scenario

      Auto-insurance claims

      A two-car collision with four seats: each driver and each driver's insurer. Every driver is the insured of one company and a claimant against the other.

      Planned

      This scenario starts after ticketing runs end to end. Two things come first.

      • One claim ledger for both routes, a claim on your own collision cover and a claim against the other insurer, so that the same loss cannot be paid twice and a policy limit cannot be used twice.
      • A review of the rules by someone who handles claims. The first rule set is California property damage, with the negligence regime as a switch.

      The worked example on the overview card: driver A is 80% at fault, damage is $10,000 to B's car and $6,000 to A's. Under pure comparative negligence B recovers $8,000 and A recovers $1,200.

      Scenario

      Online shopping

      One buyer's agent and three competing merchants, from first question to after-sales. The platform, payments, carrier and dispute rulings are played by the world.

      Built by a collaborator

      This scenario is implemented by a collaborator. It uses the same way of declaring an interface as ticketing.

      • Roles, actions, error codes and a world-time cost per call are declared once and exported as data.
      • Money is integer cents, time is UTC, validity windows are half-open.
      • Offers and consent are world objects that messages point to.

      The shared part lives in src/macbench/kernel/interface.py and src/macbench/board/interface.py.
